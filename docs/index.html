See what the Earth is doing right now.

Open a globe and the planet's week is on it: the earthquakes, the eruptions, the wildfires, the storms and the floods, each at the place it happened. Spin it, zoom in, hover and you know what, where and how long ago.

What you see

The whole planet's week, in one glance.

News tells you about the disaster everyone is talking about. EarthNow shows you everything else as well: the swarm of small quakes off Japan, the fire season moving across a continent, the volcano that has been quietly grumbling all week. It is the Earth as it actually is today.

🌍

A globe you can hold

NASA's Blue Marble on a black sky. Leave it alone and it turns slowly by itself; grab it and it is yours to spin and zoom.

☁️

The clouds, when you want them

One press lays the world's clouds over the globe, as the weather satellites saw them a few hours ago. Watch a storm system wrap around the events beneath it.

🌗

Day and night, as it is now

The real sun lights the globe. Watch dusk sweep across a continent while the city lights come up on the night side, worked out on your own machine from the time.

📍

Every event where it happened

Each one wears its own emoji, so the map reads at a glance. Earthquakes grow with their magnitude. Crowded spots gather into a count; click it and the globe zooms in until they separate.

🧭

Names the place

Hover a marker for the nearest town, its country, how far away and in which direction. No more squinting at an ocean wondering where that is.

🔎

The detail, one click away

Open an event for its time, its position, its measurement and a link to the source's own page, which opens in your browser.

⏱️

The last hour or the last week

Pick 1 hour, 6 hours, 24 hours, 3 days or 7 days. Turn categories and sources on and off from the key, which counts what is showing.

⌨️

Works from the keyboard

Every button and every event on the globe can be reached without a mouse.

Straight from the source

Nothing made up. Nothing dressed up as live.

Every event names who reported it and how old that report is. EarthNow does not guess, predict or embellish; it shows what the people who measure the planet have published, then tells you plainly when it heard.

USGS

Earthquakes from the United States Geological Survey, checked every minute.

NASA EONET

Wildfires, storms, floods, ice and more from NASA's Earth Observatory Natural Event Tracker, checked every ten minutes.

Smithsonian

The volcanoes in the Weekly Volcanic Activity Report, from the Smithsonian's Global Volcanism Program with the USGS, checked every hour.

EUMETSAT

The clouds, from EUMETSAT's world cloud map: a new picture from the ring of weather satellites every three hours, fetched only while the clouds are showing.

The globe is NASA's Blue Marble imagery, on this page as in the app; its night lights are NASA's Black Marble. Neither NASA, the USGS, the Smithsonian nor EUMETSAT endorses EarthNow. It simply shows their public reports, credited.

Calm by design

It keeps quiet and keeps working.

What you can count on

  • Offline? Still useful. It opens on the last events it received and tells you how old they are.
  • One source down? The others carry on while it quietly tries again.
  • Honest about age. A source that has gone quiet is marked stale rather than passed off as current.
  • Private. No account, no sign-in, no telemetry. It talks to the sources above and nobody else.

What it is not

  • A warning system. No alerts, no forecasts. For warnings, listen to your local authorities.
  • An archive. A week is as far back as it looks.
  • A weather service. The clouds show where cloud lay; there is no rain, wind, temperature or forecast.
  • A mapping tool. No measurements, no projections.

Install

The whole world, on your desktop.

Windows

  • Download EarthNowSetup.exe, run it and choose Install.
  • It installs for your account only, so Windows never asks for administrator rights.
  • For Windows 10 and 11. To remove it, uninstall from Settings, then Apps.

macOS

  • Download EarthNow.dmg, open it and drag EarthNow to Applications.
  • Signed and notarised by Apple, so it opens like any other app.
  • For Macs with Apple silicon.

Linux

  • Download earthnow.flatpak and install it with flatpak install --user earthnow.flatpak.
  • Flatpak fetches what it needs from Flathub by itself.
  • Tested on the latest Ubuntu LTS.
Get the latest release

It needs a graphics driver able to draw in 3D; on a machine without one, EarthNow says so rather than showing an empty window.
